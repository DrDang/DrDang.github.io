**You’re the expert.**

I heard those words early in my career during a stand-up meeting. I had just been tasked with developing an automated tool that interacted with circuit boards over JTAG. At the time, I knew absolutely nothing about it.

I looked around the room and asked, "Okay, so who is the expert I can talk to?"

Silence. Not a single hand went up. Finally, my manager looked at me and said, "You are."

That moment taught me an unintuitive lesson about the professional world: "Fake it till you make it" is not only catchy phrase but, surprisingly, it's also a mechanism for growth. We often assume experts are people who already possess total knowledge. In reality, experts are often just the people who were assigned a problem and forced to figure it out. Responsibility _creates_ competence.

(And for the record, I did become the expert. To this day, nobody knows that JTAG software better than I do.)

**The Case for Imposter Syndrome** This dynamic explains why Imposter Syndrome is so rampant. We feel like frauds because, in the beginning, we _are_ frauds. But that isn't a character flaw; it's a necessary phase of learning. It is a temporary state on the road to competence.

To thrive in that uncomfortable space, you have to check your ego at the door. You must be willing to be wrong, publically and often. It is the emotional equivalent of the SpaceX philosophy: "Move fast and break things."

I felt this when learning Japanese. The only way to get fluent was to be willing to sound foolish speaking broken sentences. I felt it learning to ice skate. If you aren't willing to risk falling on the ice, you will spend your life standing outside the rink looking in.

**The Payoff** I've joined new projects and teams where I applied this philosophy immediately. I started executing, making mistakes, correcting course, and iterating. It felt messy. I’m sure I looked like a fool more than once.

But it worked. Within months, I was winning accolades and awards.

Being the "expert" doesn't mean you know the answer right now. It means you are the one responsible for finding it. It is exhausting work, but if you can handle the bruised ego, it is the only way to truly make it.
